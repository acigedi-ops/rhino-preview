The basics

What high-risk auto insurance covers, in plain English

Coverage, limits and exclusions vary by carrier and policy. Your policy documents are what decide a claim, and we walk you through them before you buy.

What it means

Why you're "high-risk"

Insurers price by risk. Tickets, at-fault accidents, a DUI, a coverage lapse or a short driving history can move you out of a standard carrier's rules. It's not a separate type of policy: it's a regular auto policy from a carrier that accepts your record.

Washington rule

Too many tickets can suspend your license

The Department of Licensing suspends your license for 60 days if you get 6 moving violations in 12 months or 7 in 24 months, followed by a year of probation. Avoiding that suspension also protects your insurance options.

How long

Your record follows you for a few years

Most carriers look back at the last 3 to 5 years of tickets and accidents. As violations age off, you may qualify for a standard carrier, so it's worth re-shopping at renewal.

Not the same

High-risk vs. SR-22

Many high-risk drivers don't need an SR-22. You only need one if the DOL tells you to, usually after a DUI, driving uninsured or a suspension. If you do, we file it with your policy.

Avoid this

Coverage lapses

Even a short gap can raise your price, and if you have an SR-22, the insurer must tell the DOL, which can suspend your license again. Set up automatic payments or call us before a payment is missed.

No car?

Non-owner and broadform

If you don't own a car but need to drive legally or file an SR-22, a non-owner or broadform policy covers you as the driver, often for less than an owner policy.

Still worth it

Coverage beyond the minimum

Washington's minimum is 25/50/10 liability. High-risk drivers still choose PIP, underinsured motorist and, on a financed car, collision and comprehensive. We show you the price of each so you can decide.

Questions

High-risk auto questions

Can I get car insurance with a bad driving record in Washington?

Usually, yes. Some carriers specialize in high-risk drivers. Prices are higher, but we compare several to find you options.

Does Washington use a points system?

No. The DOL counts moving violations instead: 6 in 12 months or 7 in 24 months leads to a 60-day suspension. Insurers use your record to set your rate.

Do I need an SR-22?

Only if the DOL requires it, usually after a DUI, driving uninsured or a license suspension. Your DOL letter will say so. See our SR-22 page.

How long will I pay high-risk rates?

It depends on the carrier and the violation. Most look back 3 to 5 years, so prices often improve as your record ages. We re-shop at renewal.

I'm new to the US. Can I get insured?

Often, yes. Some carriers accept drivers with a foreign or new Washington license, usually at a higher rate at first. Tell us your situation, and we'll check which carriers will write you.

What happens if I let my insurance lapse?

A lapse can raise your next price, and with an SR-22 the DOL is notified and can suspend your license. Read our non-owner SR-22 guide or license reinstatement guide if you're getting back on the road.
