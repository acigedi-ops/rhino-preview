The basics

What roofing contractor insurance covers, in plain English

Coverage, limits and exclusions vary by carrier and policy. Your policy documents are what decide a claim, and we walk you through them before you buy.

Why it's harder

Not every carrier writes roofers

Falls, fire from torch work and water getting into a home while the roof is open make roofing a high-risk class. Some carriers decline roofers, and others limit roof height, stories or hot work. We match you with carriers that fit the work you do.

Washington L&I

Registering as a roofing contractor

To register with L&I you need general liability of at least $200,000 public liability and $50,000 property damage, or a $250,000 combined single limit, plus a contractor bond: $15,000 for specialty contractors or $30,000 for general contractors. Many general contractors require $1 million or more.

Core coverage

General liability

Injury to other people and damage to their property caused by your work, such as a shingle bundle falling on a car. Your own faulty work is usually not covered; damage it causes to other property may be, depending on the policy.

Western Washington

Rain and an open roof

Water getting in while a roof is torn off is one of the most common roofing claims in our climate. Some roofing policies limit or exclude it, so we check this wording before you buy.

After the job

Completed operations

A leak that damages a home's interior after you finish can be a completed operations claim. It may pay for the resulting damage, but usually not to redo the roof itself.

Your crew

Workers' comp through L&I

In Washington, workers' comp comes from L&I, not private insurers, and roofing pays some of the highest rates. General liability doesn't cover your own workers' injuries.

Trucks and tools

Commercial auto and equipment

Crew trucks, trailers and dump trailers need commercial auto. Ladders, nail guns, compressors and hoists need tools and equipment coverage, which general liability doesn't include.

Subcontractors

Certificates from your subs

Carriers often require certificates of insurance from any roofers you subcontract to. Uninsured subs can be added to your premium at audit.

Questions

Roofing contractor questions

Why is roofing insurance harder to get than other trades?

Height, fire risk from torch work and water damage claims make roofing high-risk. Some carriers decline it, and the ones that write it often limit stories or hot work. Telling us exactly what you do helps us find the right fit.

What insurance do I need to register as a roofer in Washington?

General liability of at least $200,000 / $50,000 or a $250,000 combined single limit, plus a contractor bond, usually $15,000 for a specialty contractor. See our bond page.

Does general liability cover a leak after I finish a roof?

It may cover the damage the leak causes inside the home, under completed operations. It usually won't pay to redo your own work. The details depend on the policy wording.

Is water damage covered while the roof is open?

It depends on the policy. Some roofing policies cover it, and others limit or exclude it. We check this before you buy, because it matters in Western Washington.

Do I need workers' comp as a roofer?

If you have employees, yes, through L&I. Sole owners without employees can usually opt in. General liability never covers your own workers.

How much does roofing insurance cost?

It depends on your revenue or payroll, the type of roofing, roof height, torch work and your claims history. We compare carriers that write roofers so you can see real prices. See general liability.
