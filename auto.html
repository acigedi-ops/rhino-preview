The basics

What auto insurance covers, in plain English

Coverage, limits and exclusions vary by carrier and policy. Your policy documents are what decide a claim, and we walk you through them before you buy.

Required in WA

Bodily injury liability

Pays for injuries you cause to other people, and your legal defense if they sue. It never pays for your own injuries. Washington's minimum is $25,000 per person and $50,000 per accident, which a serious crash can easily exceed.

Required in WA

Property damage liability

Pays for the other car, fence or building you damage. The state minimum is $10,000, less than the cost of many cars on the road, so most drivers choose more.

Must be offered in WA

Uninsured / underinsured motorist

Pays for your injuries, and with the property damage option your car, when the at-fault driver has no insurance, too little, or drives off. Carriers must offer it, and you can only turn it down in writing.

Must be offered in WA

Personal injury protection (PIP)

Washington's no-fault medical coverage for you and your passengers, whoever caused the crash. The basic level pays up to $10,000 in medical bills, $10,000 in lost income (up to $200 a week), $5,000 for household help and $2,000 for funeral costs. You can reject it only in writing.

Optional

Medical payments

A separate, optional coverage that pays medical and funeral bills for you and your passengers regardless of fault. Unlike PIP, it does not pay lost wages or household help. Some carriers offer it to add on top of PIP.

Your car

Collision

Repairs your own car after a crash with another vehicle or an object, whoever was at fault, minus your deductible. Lenders and leasing companies require it.

Your car

Comprehensive

Theft, vandalism, fire, falling objects, glass, hitting an animal and weather damage, minus your deductible. Liability plus collision and comprehensive is what people call full coverage.

Named driver

Broadform

Liability that follows one named driver in any car they drive, including their own, but not other people who drive their car. It has no collision or comprehensive, so it won't satisfy a lender.

A filing, not a policy

SR-22

A form your insurer files with the Washington Department of Licensing to prove you carry liability insurance. Usually required after a DUI, driving uninsured or a license suspension.

Questions

Auto questions

What are Washington's minimum auto insurance limits?

Liability of 25/50/10: $25,000 per person and $50,000 per accident for injuries, and $10,000 for property damage. Carriers must also offer PIP and underinsured motorist coverage. See our guide to Washington minimums.

What is the difference between PIP and medical payments?

PIP is Washington's no-fault coverage. It pays medical bills plus lost income, household help and funeral costs, and carriers must offer it. Medical payments is optional and pays medical and funeral bills only. Some drivers carry both.

Does full coverage mean everything is covered?

No. It usually means liability plus collision and comprehensive. Rental cars, towing, gap coverage and custom equipment are separate options, and every policy has exclusions and deductibles.

Do I need uninsured motorist coverage?

It is optional, but it is often the only coverage that pays for your injuries when the other driver has no insurance or too little. Rejecting it has to be done in writing.

Can I get an SR-22 if I don't own a car?

Yes. A non-owner or broadform policy covers you as the driver, and we can file the SR-22 on either one. See our SR-22 page.

Can I get insured with tickets, an accident or a DUI?

Usually, yes, through carriers that write high-risk drivers. See high-risk auto insurance.

What should I do after an accident?

Get to safety, exchange information and report the claim to your insurance company as soon as you can. Our after-a-crash guide has a checklist.
